# Prisoner Codes (Ciphers)

Watched a movie or TV where someone tapped on a surface or pipe to send morse code?<br>
Well morse uses Carrier Wave (CW) and requires a 1 count duration signal and a 3 count duration signal.
A space between letters is 3 count silence and words are spaced with 7 count silence.<br>
A tap on a surface is instant, no duration.
So regular morse code doesn't work in movies and TV or some real life situations.
The rhythm/beat/timing is needs to be consistent.
The duration between taps indicates the signal, a short pause (1 count) after a tap is a di, and a longer pause (3 count) is a dah.

## Others

Tap code (Polybius square): used by prisoners of war.
Letters sit on a 5×5 grid (C and K shared) and are sent as a group of taps for the row, a pause, then a group for the column.
It needs only tap count and pause length, with no dit/dah distinction.
